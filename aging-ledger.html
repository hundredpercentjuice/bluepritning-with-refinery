ORDER-TO-CASH · OPEN ITEM ANALYSIS

100 invoices are carrying $165.2M of Meridian Silicon's uncollected receivables.

Every one of them is seven figures. Every one is untouched by a single cash application. And on average, they've been outstanding for 128 days — nearly twice as long as MSI takes to collect an invoice that actually gets paid. This is a walkthrough of the 100 largest open items in Accounts Receivable: how big the pile is, who is holding it, how old it has gotten, and what a collections team should do about it Monday morning.

EXHIBIT 1

These aren't the oldest debts — they're the biggest

If the top 100 were simply "whatever has been forgotten longest," its aging profile would skew older than the rest of the book. It doesn't. Side by side against all 918 open items, the $165.2M cohort ages almost identically to the wider population — concentrated in the 91–180 day band, with nothing in the current bucket and nothing over a year. Size, not neglect, is what put these invoices on this list.

EXHIBIT 2

The same five accounts run both books

Four of MSI's five largest revenue customers — Apex Mobile, Helia Devices, Aurion Distribution, Vantek Components — are also its four largest holders of aged receivables inside this cohort. That's the structural risk in one sentence: the customers MSI can least afford to press hard on are the ones sitting on the most overdue cash.

EXHIBIT 4

Reading it

01

Four calls cover more than half the cohort. Apex Mobile, Helia Devices, Aurion Distribution, and Vantek Components together hold $83.1M of the $165.2M — a single coordinated outreach to four credit contacts would move more of the needle than working the other 96 invoices combined.

02

The 91–180 day band is where the money actually is. $133.6M of the $165.2M (80.9%) sits in that single band. These invoices are well past terms but not yet written-off candidates — the highest-leverage collections window is right now, before they roll into the 181–365 day tier.

03

Nothing here is a service or billing dispute. Zero partial payments across all 918 open items rules out the usual "short-paid, needs reconciliation" excuse. These are invoices customers have simply not paid — a credit and collections conversation, not an AR-ops cleanup.

04

128 days average vs. 69.3 days to collect. MSI's paid population already resolves slower than a ~45–47 day top-quartile benchmark. The top 100 open items are running at nearly double MSI's own already-slow average — this is the tail dragging the average down, not noise around it.

None of this cohort's four biggest holders can be pressed the way a small, low-strategic-value account can. Apex Mobile alone is 21.8% of MSI's billed revenue — a credit hold on that account is a revenue decision, not just a collections one. Any remediation plan here has to run through Sales and Finance jointly, not AR Ops alone.
METHOD

How this was built

Open item
A row in AR.AR_PAYMENT_SCHEDULES_ALL with STATUS = 'OP'. Customer name resolved two hops out — RA_CUSTOMER_TRX_ALL.BILL_TO_CUSTOMER_ID → HZ_CUST_ACCOUNTS → HZ_PARTIES.PARTY_NAME.
Ranking
All 918 open items ordered by AMOUNT_DUE_REMAINING descending; top 100 taken as the cohort. Nothing in the population is partially paid, so AMOUNT_DUE_REMAINING equals AMOUNT_DUE_ORIGINAL on every row.
Aging
Days past due computed as of 2026‑09‑17 minus DUE_DATE. This is a balance-method exposure snapshot, not a DSO calculation — AR_RECEIVABLE_APPLICATIONS_ALL links only to already-closed schedules in this dataset, so realized collection speed (69.3 days, cited above) is measured separately, on the paid population only.
Window
Full transaction history, Jan 2024 – May 2026 (28 GL periods). This is a point-in-time register of what's owed today, not a trailing-window metric.
Revenue cross-ref
"Top revenue customer" tags reference MSI's all-history billed-revenue concentration (Apex Mobile 21.8%, Helia Devices 12.0%, Aurion Distribution 9.7%, Vantek Components 9.2%, Cipher Computing Corp 2.5%), computed independently from AR.RA_CUSTOMER_TRX_LINES_ALL.
Source
Oracle EBS (Autonomous Data Warehouse), single operating unit (ORG_ID 204). Queried via the TextQL refinery CLI on 2026‑09‑17.

Meridian Silicon Inc. (MSI) is a fictional-but-production-shaped fabless semiconductor demo estate.
